\subsection*{Summary of Contributions and Significance}
Numerical differentiation of noise-contaminated signals is a classic ill-posed inverse problem fundamental to scientific computing, control theory, and data-driven dynamical system discovery. Standard methods, including finite difference stencils and local polynomial filters (such as Savitzky-Golay), suffer from catastrophic variance amplification of order $\mathcal{O}(\sigma^2 / \Delta x^2)$ as grid spacing vanishes.

In this work, I develop the theory of \textbf{Integral Derivative Manifolds (IDM)}, establishing a rigorous differential-geometric formulation that treats scale $\epsilon$ as an intrinsic Riemannian coordinate:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact Spatial Commutativity and Linearity:} I prove that the integral derivative operator commutes identically with spatial translation and reproduces exact linear fields with zero discretization artifact.
    \item \textbf{Surface Geometry and Gaussian Curvature Rigidity:} I define the 2D Riemannian scale surface $\mathcal{M}_f = \{(x, \epsilon, \mathcal{D}_\epsilon f)\}$ and prove that its Gaussian curvature $K(x, \epsilon) \equiv 0$ vanishes identically for linear fields, while developing sharp curvature spikes at signal singularities.
    \item \textbf{Colored (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck) Noise Theory:} For correlated noise with finite correlation length $\tau_c$, I derive the closed-form variance and prove that temporal correlation softens the noise singularity from $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-3})$ to $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})$.
    \item \textbf{Optimal AMISE Scale Optimization and Two-Pass Pilot Calibration:} I derive the closed-form MSE-optimal scale $\epsilon^*(x) \asymp (\sigma^2 \Delta x)^{1/5}$ and establish a practical two-pass algorithm with automatic variance estimation.
\end{enumerate}

Extensive empirical evaluations across benchmark signals (Doppler chirp, shock transitions, and chaotic Lorenz dynamics) demonstrate up to a \textbf{246$\times$ error reduction} over central finite differences and consistent superiority over Savitzky-Golay and Tikhonov regularization.
