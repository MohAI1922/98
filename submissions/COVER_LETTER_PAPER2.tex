\subsection*{Summary of Contributions and Significance}
Estimating spatial gradients and directional rates of change over irregular graphs, point clouds, and Riemannian submanifolds is a core challenge in graph signal processing, geometric deep learning, and 3D perception. Classical network finite differences $\frac{y_j - y_i}{d_{ij}}$ suffer from an intrinsic \emph{density divergence problem}: as sampling density increases ($N \to \infty$), the nearest neighbor distance $d_{ij} \to 0$, causing variance to explode as $\mathcal{O}(\sigma^2 / d_{ij}^2) \to \infty$. Conversely, Moving Least Squares (MLS) relies on ambient Euclidean chords that short-circuit across surface folds and thin sheets.

This manuscript resolves both pathologies by formulating **Graph Integral Derivative Manifolds (G-IDM)**:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Local Metric Moment Tensor:} I construct the symmetric positive-definite tensor $\mathbf{M}_\epsilon(v_i) = \sum_{j} w_\epsilon(d_{\mathcal{G}}) \mathbf{v}_{ij} \mathbf{v}_{ij}^\top$ weighted by intrinsic geodesic graph shortest paths, proving a strict spectral lower bound with exact closed-form Euler Beta moment constants.
    \item \textbf{Exact Affine Invariance:} I prove mathematically that G-IDM reproduces affine fields ($\nabla_\epsilon^{\mathcal{G}} f \equiv \mathbf{a}$) with zero truncation error on arbitrary, severely irregular, and anisotropic point clouds.
    \item \textbf{Submanifold Truncation Convergence:} For curved $d$-dimensional Riemannian submanifolds $\mathcal{M} \subset \mathbb{R}^D$, I prove clean $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)$ convergence, showing via the Gauss equation that intrinsic curvature is completely absorbed by the second fundamental form.
    \item \textbf{Topological Geodesic Routing vs. Chordal Short-Circuits:} I prove that on folded manifolds (e.g., Swiss roll), ambient Euclidean MLS incurs an irreducible $\mathcal{O}(1)$ bias from cross-fold bridging, whereas geodesic G-IDM preserves topological consistency.
    \item \textbf{Stochastic Density Inversion on Random Geometric Graphs:} I prove that the noise variance of G-IDM scales as $\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{N \epsilon^{d+2}}\right) \asymp \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sigma^2}{k \epsilon^2}\right)$, proving that increasing network node density monotonically *suppresses* estimation noise—reversing the classical finite difference explosion.
\end{enumerate}

Extensive empirical evaluations across 2D irregular sensor networks ($N = 800$), 3D spherical point clouds ($N = 1000$), and folded Swiss roll manifolds ($N = 1200$) demonstrate up to a \textbf{7.3$\times$ error reduction} over graph finite differences and a \textbf{6.3$\times$ error reduction} over chordal Moving Least Squares.
